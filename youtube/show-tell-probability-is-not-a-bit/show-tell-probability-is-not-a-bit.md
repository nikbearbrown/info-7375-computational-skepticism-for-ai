## show-tell-probability-is-not-a-bit-slate

Jambo, this is Liam Inferbear with a short film for Info 7375 Computational Skepticism for AI. You might ask, is the answer true or false? Probability asks a different question. How confident should I be given what I know? For terms, sample space, every possible outcome. Event, a subset of those outcomes. Probability measure, a number assigned to each event under three rules. Prior, the belief you bring before new evidence arrives. Most of us came up through a Boolean education. A bit is one or zero. A program runs or it does not. A test passes or fails. Even uncertainty often arrives as a small wobble around a yes or no answer. Probability is a different animal. Its output is a degree of belief. A number between zero and one that says how confident you should be given what you currently know. Two engineers can see exactly the same evidence, assigned different probabilities, and both reason correctly. They arrived with different priors. Probability is conditional on an information state. That shift should bother you a little. Boolean instinct works in deterministic systems. It misleads when you work from a model rather than the world, and every machine learning system, AI deployment and statistical inference is a model. A sample space omega is every possible outcome. For a diet is one through six, an event is any subset. The event even lifts the tiles to four and six from the omega tray. The first axiom is non-negativity. P of a is at least zero for every event a. A probability bar can begin at the floor, but it never extends below zero. The second axiom is normalization. P of omega equals one. Something has to happen. Sick or healthy exhausts the patient's sample space, so together their bars fill the whole height. The third axiom is additivity. For disjoint and B, P of a union B equals P of a plus P of B. Even in five cannot both occur on one die roll, so their bars stack without overlap. Those three rules are the entire foundation. Conditional probability, base theorem and the central limit theorem are derived from them. When a formula becomes mysterious, return to the three supports. Two consequences follow. P of the empty set is zero. Nothing happening has probability zero, and P of a complement is one minus P of a. For the die, the complement of even is the three outcomes left behind. One, three, and five. The event and its complement partition the omega tray and fill the same total of one. Every probability calculation in this book follows from these rules. The result is not a bit. It is your degree of belief given what you know, which is why two careful engineers can differ.
