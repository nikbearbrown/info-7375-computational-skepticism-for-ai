## Bear's words (2026-09-27, dictated; lightly cleaned, as quoted in the brief)

> Show and tell video, Liam persona, on Immanuel Kant, and how we need some humility in our perspective.
>
> Immanuel Kant is the closest single match, and it's not close. Phenomena (the world as it appears to a mind structured by space, time, and causal categories) vs. noumena (the thing-in-itself, forever inaccessible). Gravity as something inferred from effects rather than directly seen is almost a textbook Kantian example: you never perceive causality itself, you infer it, because causality is a category your mind imposes on experience rather than a feature you're reading off the world. Kant's response to "will tools ever close the gap" is a flat no — not because instruments are too weak, but because the very form of any possible experience (space, time) is mind-supplied. That's a stronger claim than mine; I think the gap is contingent-but-permanent (finite minds, expanding tools, asymptotic), not structurally guaranteed shut like Kant does.
>
> The idea is that we only see a slice of the universe. And we should be aware not only of what we can't see, but that there are things we can't see.

## The brief's content arc (from `../SHOW-TELL-KANT.md`, "The content")

1. Phenomena vs noumena, stated as Kant states it (Critique, B xxvi–xxvii; the Transcendental Aesthetic), verbatim only from a public-domain translation (Meiklejohn), with the B-edition page.
2. Space, time and causality are mind-supplied: space and time the forms of sensibility, causality a category of the understanding. Drawn as a lens or frame the mind brings to everything it sees.
3. Gravity, the worked example: the apple falls, the orbit bends, the cause is inferred. Historically Hume's problem, which Kant answers; the *Prolegomena* (1783) preface, "dogmatic slumber", verbatim if fetched from a public-domain translation (Carus 1902).
4. Will better tools close the gap? Kant says no, in principle: a better telescope gives a sharper slice, not a bigger one.
5. Bear's own position, stated as his: the gap is permanent but contingent (finite minds, ever-better tools, approaching without arriving). Attributed clearly to Bear, spoken and labelled; never presented as Kant's or as settled. The film holds both.
6. The humility: two kinds of not-seeing (what's outside the slice; that there is an outside). The lantern in a large dark room. One AI beat: a model's confidence is about its slice.
7. Your Turn: one thing you or a model is confident about; what you observe, what you infer, one thing outside the slice you cannot observe at all. Two checks.
