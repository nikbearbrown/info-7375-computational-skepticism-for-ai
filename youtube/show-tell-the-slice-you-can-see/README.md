# You Only See a Slice

**What this is.** A show-tell film (3:31) for INFO 7375, Computational Skepticism for AI: a companion to the three "moves" films (Descartes, Hume, Popper), on Immanuel Kant and humility about what we can observe. Liam, in for Bear, says it is a short film for the course, then makes Bear's point: we only ever see a slice of the world, and we should know both what lies outside the slice and that there is an outside at all. **Why.** Bear ordered it on 2026-09-27 (`../SHOW-TELL-KANT.md`): "Immanuel Kant is the closest single match, and it's not close." **Found.** Kant says the film's claims in his own words, through public-domain translations: the thing in itself we can think but never know (*Critique*, B xxvi); an appearance without something that appears would be absurd (B xxvi–xxvii); space and time are forms of our sensibility and cause and effect a category of the understanding (B xxv, A80/B106); Hume's suggestion "first interrupted my dogmatic slumber" (*Prolegomena*, Carus, Ak. 4:260); and better instruments bring us "not one step nearer" to things in themselves (A43/B60). Where Bear parts from Kant, the film says so aloud ("This is Bear's view, not Kant's") and on screen ("Bear's view"): Kant's gap is shut in principle; Bear's is contingent-but-permanent, asymptotic. The film holds both.
