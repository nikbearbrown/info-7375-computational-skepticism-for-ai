Every instrument hands you a sharper slice of the world. The real question is whether you know it's a slice. For terms, phenomena, the world as it appears to a mind, new mena, things as they are in themselves. Category, a concept the mind supplies to every experience like cause and effect. And the slice, the part of the world you can actually observe. The idea comes from a manual Kant in the critique of pure reason from 1781 with a second edition in 1787. Kant split the world in two. Phenomena, things as they appear to a mind, new mena, things as they are in themselves. Everything you have ever seen is on the near side of that line. We know appearances, the thing in itself we can think but never know. In Kant's words, while we surrender the power of cognizing, we still reserve the power of thinking objects as things in themselves. And he never said the thing isn't there. An appearance without something that appears he wrote would be absurd. Where does the frame come from, not from the world from us? Space and time, Kant said, are the forms of our sensibility. The shape every experience must take before we can have it. And cause and effect is one of the categories. A concept the understanding brings to experience, not a feature it reads off the world. Everything you will ever see arrives already framed. Take gravity, you never see it. You see the apple fall, you see the orbit bend. The cause you infer, David Hume had made that problem. So we'll better instruments close the gap. Kant says no and not because instruments are weak. Any possible experience still comes through space, time and the categories. And we should not thereby advance one step nearer to a knowledge of the Constitution of objects as things in themselves. A better telescope gives you a sharper slice, not a bigger one. Bear doesn't go that far, this is Bear's view not Kant's. The gap is permanent but contingent, finite minds, ever better tools approaching without arriving. Asymptotic, Kant's gap is shut in principle by the very form of experience. Bear's is shut in practice by how far finite minds and their tools can reach. Keep both in mind what's outside the patch and that there is an outside. One line about AI, a model's confidence is confidence about its slice. The data it was trained on, the inputs it was given.
