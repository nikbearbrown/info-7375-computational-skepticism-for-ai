# SOURCES — show-tell-the-slice-you-can-see

**What this is.** Every source the film draws on, with where the local copy lives. **Why.** The film states Kant's positions in Kant's own words (through public-domain translations only), with edition and page references, and keeps Bear's own view separate from Kant's; each claim is checked against its primary, fetched raw on 2026-09-27, not against a summary. **Found.** Meiklejohn's 1855 translation of the *Critique* (public domain) carries the two claims the film needs verbatim: the thing in itself can be thought but not known (B xxvi), and clearer perception brings us "not one step nearer" to things in themselves (A43/B60). Carus's 1902 translation of the *Prolegomena* (public domain) carries the "dogmatic slumber" line verbatim (Preface, Ak. 4:260). No copyrighted translation (Kemp Smith 1929, Guyer/Wood 1998) is quoted or held locally; the A/B page numbers are the standard pagination of the two German editions, located by passage.
