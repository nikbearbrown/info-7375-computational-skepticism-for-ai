**What it is.** A `tldr` film. Liam says what the film is about and why it matters (two TL;DR cards). The hesitant writer corrects "calibrated for every patient" to "calibrated group by group", and four terms land. Then comes one LEARN section in pure Manim, built on the chapter's illustrative subgroup table:
1. The global 0.018 splits into five rows.
2. Three rows climb past twice the global.
3. Averaging the rows gives the wrong number.
4. The columns resize to their patient counts, and the rare-disease row becomes a sliver.
5. A predict beat.
6. The sliver pours into a hairline (it can never move the global by a hundredth), then is lifted out onto its own 95 at 0.103.
7. Only then comes the name: subgroup calibration error.

**Why.** A report with one global calibration number checks the model on average, not for the patient in front of you. The smallest groups are exactly the ones the average cannot hear.
