# PEDAGOGY — claude-liam-bnb-01-build-and-suspect

## Act structure
Series opener (7 beats, no B04). Cold open (B00) delivers the one idea — builder as worst auditor — and the full six-episode arc overview. B01 names the five build options and the four-word rule: small, real, yours, fully understood. B02 constructs the build note with the pre-registered suspect as its last sentence. B03 establishes the prediction-lock — dated, before running, graded on order not correctness. BVDT restates the key rules and closes with the series' thesis. BHTF hands off the prediction-lock drafting prompt with both instrument fill-in brackets named. BOUT closes.

## Cold open law
Opens on the assignment in one sentence and the one idea immediately: the builder-as-worst-auditor framing is stated in the first minute, not deferred. The six-episode arc is previewed as a single spine so students know where they are going before any episode-specific content begins. B00's `output` array names three deliverable-level items so the ClaudeComposerAsk artifact is not abstract.

## Gap formula
Gap: students understand skeptical methods in the abstract but apply them to examples they didn't build, which produces surface-level engagement. Bridge: the build is small, real, and theirs — which forces all instruments to reveal actual decisions the student made, not hypothetical ones. Close: a dated prediction-lock written before running produces a before/after comparison that cannot be faked.

## Utility lint
Five options are enumerated with a single distinguishing sentence each — not definitions, behaviorally distinguishable descriptions. The four-word rule (small, real, yours, fully understood) is given a test: "can you trace every prediction back to the row?" The build note's suspect is specified by example (label threshold, join, feature, window) and distinguished from vague alternatives ('data quality', 'accuracy'). The prediction-lock grading rule is explicit: order, not correctness.

## Vocabulary / register
**Artifact** — the student's build (classifier, dataset, dashboard, agent recipe, or prompt system); first defined in B01. **Build note** — three-part document naming what the artifact does, its data, and the pre-registered suspect; defined in B02. **Pre-registered suspect** — the specific decision the builder was least confident about; named first as a concept in B00, operationalized in B02. **Prediction-lock** — a dated, written prediction of what an instrument will find, made before running; defined and rules given in B03. These four terms are used consistently across all six episodes.

## Honesty
The claim "a builder is the least reliable auditor of their own build" is stated as the one idea and returned to at the verdict. It is not presented as a flaw to be corrected but as a structural condition to be instrumentalized. The grading note — wrong prediction-lock is a finding, not a penalty — is accurate and prevents students from treating the exercise as a correctness test.

## Length law
