# Doubt What Your Work Depends On

**What this is.** A show-tell film (2:34) for INFO 7375, Computational Skepticism for AI: Film 1 of the three-moves batch Bear ordered on 2026-09-27 (`../SHOW-TELL-THREE-MOVES.md`). Liam, in for Bear, opens with "Salaam", says it is a short film for the course, and makes one point: you can't doubt everything, so aim the doubt by risk. Descartes, in the *Meditations* (1641), tore down every belief he could find any reason to doubt, once in his life, and said it was safe only because he was after knowledge, not action. This course borrows his move as one question: what would have to be true for this to be wrong? That question costs time, and Descartes knew it: in the *Discourse on the Method* (1637) he kept a provisional code to live by while he doubted, like a traveller lost in a forest who walks one straight line and acts on what is most probable. Then Bear's rule: ask how likely a claim is to be wrong and what it would cost if it were. The chair you sit on goes in the just-act corner; the triage score, the medication dose and the number your job depends on get the doubt, in proportion to the cost. Your Turn: list five claims your work relied on this week, rank them by the cost of being wrong, and run the question on the top one or two only. **Why.** Bear's words: "You can't doubt everything, but you should doubt what your job depends on — health, important things." **Found.** Descartes' texts support everything the film puts in his mouth, and they limit doubt in practice for a different reason than Bear does (action can't wait, not cost). So the film credits the "what would make it wrong?" question to the course, and risk-weighted doubt to Bear, never to Descartes (`FACTCHECK.md` rows 13, 30).
