**What this is.** Every claim the film speaks or shows, checked against its source: `SOURCE.md` (Bear's words from `../SHOW-TELL-THREE-MOVES.md` and chapter 1 of INFO 7375, lines 33–49 and 97, verbatim) and, for everything Descartes is said to have written, his own texts, fetched raw on 2026-09-27 into `sources/`: the *Meditations* in the Latin of the first edition (Gutenberg #23306, `sources/meditationes-latin-pg23306.txt`) and in John Veitch's English (Wikisource, `sources/meditations-I-veitch-wikisource.wikitext`, `sources/meditations-synopsis-veitch-wikisource.wikitext`); the *Discourse on the Method*, Part III, Veitch (Gutenberg #59, `sources/discourse-on-method-veitch-pg59.txt`); the *Principles of Philosophy*, Part I, arts. 1–3, Veitch (Gutenberg #4391, `sources/principles-of-philosophy-veitch-pg4391.txt`). No WebFetch summary was used. **Result:** PASS, with one framing CORRECTED and one attribution kept explicit. (1) **Whose move is it?** The brief puts "what would have to be true for a claim to be wrong" in Descartes' mouth. His own method is stronger and different: reject every opinion with the least ground for doubt, once in his life, and treat the doubtful as false (Meditations I ¶1–2; Principles I.1–2). The chapter itself says it is teaching "the move", not Cartesian philosophy. So the film says Descartes tore down every belief he could doubt, and that "this course borrows his move as one question". (2) **Risk is Bear's rule, not Descartes'.** Descartes limits doubt for a different reason: action can't wait (Discourse III, second maxim; Principles I.3), and the Meditations' doubt is safe only because "the end I now seek is not action but knowledge" (Med. I ¶11). The film says each thing as its source says it and never claims Descartes weighed doubt by cost; the risk picture is labelled "not measured" and spoken as "a way of thinking, not a measured model". Everything Descartes is said to have written was found in the text and quoted in the rows below.
