## show-tell-doubt-what-matters-slate

Salam, this is Liam in for bear with a short film for info 7375 computational skepticism for AI. You might think a good skeptic doubts everything. You can't and you shouldn't try. The real question is what to doubt. Three terms, radical doubt, asking what would have to be true for a claim to be wrong. Risk how likely a claim is to be wrong and what it costs if it is. And a provisional code rules you act by while you're still doubting. In his meditations published in 1641, Descartes set out once in his life to tear down every belief he could find any reason to doubt and rebuild from the foundations. He says it was safe to go that far because he was after knowledge, not action. This course borrows his move as one question you can ask of any claim. What would have to be true for this to be wrong? The answer isn't a mood, it's a list of things you can go and check. But you can't run that question on everything. Every check costs time. Whatever claim you meet and you never get to act and the work just waits. Descartes saw this in his discourse on the method from 1637. He says that while you pull down your house to rebuild it, you need another house to live in. So while he doubted, he kept a provisional code, a few rules to act by. One rule, a traveler lost in a forest shouldn't wander from side to side or stand still. Pick a direction and walk straight and you'll likely end up somewhere better than the middle of the forest. You can't tell what's true and you have to act, act on what's most probable. So aim the doubt, ask two things about a claim. How likely is it to be wrong and what would it cost if it were? Together that's risk. Treat this as a way of thinking, not a measured model. The chair you're sitting on has held you for years and if it failed you'd land on the floor. Don't doubt it. Sit down. Most claims in a working day live in this corner. Act on them and fix them if they break. A few claims land over here. A triage score on a patient, a medication dose, the number your job depends on. If one of those is wrong, someone pays for it. Those get the doubt. The rule is proportion. The more it costs to be wrong, the more doubt a claim gets. Cheap to be wrong, act and watch. Costly to be wrong, stop and ask what would have to be true for it to be wrong before the world answers for you.
