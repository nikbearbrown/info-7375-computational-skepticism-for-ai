In 1980, John Searle locked a man in a room with a rulebook and had him answer letters in fluent Chinese — a language he couldn't read. His point: the man understood nothing. Forty-five years later, did we go and build that room?

This video traces the Chinese Room from Turing's imitation game through Searle's original argument, the strongest objections (the Systems Reply, the Robot Reply), and then asks the hard question: do modern large language models pass — or did they just build a much bigger room?
