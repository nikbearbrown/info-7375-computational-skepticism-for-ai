## Things noticed on the deck itself (not changed; for Bear)

- Slide 'Classical, frequentist, Bayesian', step 3: the caption says the evidence 'makes the hypothesis 4 times as likely', but the page multiplies the ODDS by 4 (likelihood ratio 4). The probabilities shown go 0.10 to 0.31 (3.1 times) and 0.50 to 0.80 (1.6 times), so '4 times as likely' is not true of either bar. Suggested wording: 'it multiplies the odds by 4'. The narration avoids the phrase.
- Slide 'Classical, frequentist, Bayesian', step 1: the ball labelled 'yellow' is filled red (the highlight colour) while the ball labelled 'red' is drawn white, so the colour names and the fills disagree. The narration says 'the one labelled yellow highlighted'.
- Slide 'Conditioning, the chain rule, independence', step 1: 'The flag is common, so it lowers the estimate' is an incomplete reason. A common flag does not by itself lower the estimate; it is lower because flagged cases are high-acuity less often than cases overall (0.30 is below 0.76 x 0.45 = 0.342). The chapter gives the fuller reason ('the flag is common and most flagged cases are not serious'). The narration states the number and not the cause.
- Slide 'Sample space, event and the axioms', step 3: the bar pieces are labelled 0.17 but the line reads '6 x 0.167 = 1.000'; 6 x 0.167 is 1.002 and 6 x 0.17 is 1.02. Rounded values are shown as an exact product.
- Evidence badges do not match every step: 'Central limit theorem, heavy tails, loss' is badged Simulation (seeded) but step 3 (999 errors at $10, one at $500,000) is illustrative data; 'Calibration and the decorative number' step 1 (700, 400, 900) is the chapter's illustrative example under a Simulation badge; 'Say it exactly' is badged Computed although its inputs are the illustrative chain numbers, the seeded simulation and the illustrative costs; 'Look-alike pairs' is badged Illustrative although pair 1 is the computed 99% test. The narration qualifies the numbers step by step.
- Slides 'Say it exactly' (row 3) and the checklist's last line: the vague claim is '94% confident' but the exact version reports 0.97 stated and 0.85 actual; 94% appears nowhere in the simulated data, so a viewer may look for where 94 went.
- Slide 'Distribution shift and Hume's problem': the term card 'Distribution shift' appears at step 1, when the chart shows only the ten batches before the shift; the shift itself is drawn at step 2, which adds no term.
