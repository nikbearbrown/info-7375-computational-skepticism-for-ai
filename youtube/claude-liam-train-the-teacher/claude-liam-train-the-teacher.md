# Train the Teacher: I Had My Own Essay Audited. Four Sentences Didn't Survive.

In May I published an argument about phone bans, EdTech, and where school
technology money should actually go. Then I did something most people who
publish an argument never do — I had it attacked, under instructions to fix or
kill every number in it.

This film is what came back. The recommendation survived. Four of the sentences
carrying it did not, including the statistic the essay opened on. And the study
that would actually prove the central claim turns out not to exist.

Five corrections, shown on screen as corrections rather than quietly swapped in:

• The Florida suspension figure is **in-school** suspensions among **Black
  students** — not "Black boys" — with no significant effect on out-of-school
  suspensions.
• The $46.23-per-student result bought a **package** — software, training, a
  warm-up year, and ongoing support — not teacher training alone.
• "Not one state defines educational purposes" is disproved by Texas and
  New York.
• The 30/70 spending rule is a recommendation from **2002**, not a current
  measurement.
• "This is what the evidence shows" is properly **a defensible bet** — the
  direct trial has never been run.

The argument comes out of this better than it went in. That is what an audit is
for.

## Your turn

Take something you have published — a post, a memo, a proposal — and don't ask
Claude whether it's good. Paste this instead:

> Here is something I published. Run an adversarial evidence audit: decompose it
> into separate claims, rule on each, verify every number against its actual
> source, and tell me which claims are findings and which are bets.

That last question is the one that changes how you write.

## What the evidence actually says

• Teacher coaching, pooled across 60 causal studies: 0.49 SD on instructional
  practice, 0.18 SD on student achievement — and the effect shrinks as programmes
  scale (Kraft, Blazar & Hogan, 2018).
• A 93-hour randomised maths PD programme raised teacher knowledge and some
  practices, and moved student achievement not at all (IES).
• Live AI assistance to tutors: +4 percentage points on lesson-topic mastery,
  +9 for students of the lowest-rated tutors — with no significant end-of-year
  difference (Wang et al., Tutor CoPilot, working paper).
