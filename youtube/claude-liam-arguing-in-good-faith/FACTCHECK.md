## Notes on deliberate deviations from the source carousel

### Note A — GOOD FAITH replaces BE RESPECTFUL

The source card: *"Comprehending complex layers of systemic racism and confronting one's own
guilt in maintaining a racist status-quo takes a lot of emotional energy and courage."*

This embeds a contested political conclusion (that the interlocutor holds guilt for a status quo)
as a conduct prerequisite. Embedding a contested conclusion as a premise inside a civility rule
is an instance of begging the question (Copi & Cohen §7.6). The rewrite preserves the intended
norm (extend grace, assume effort) without smuggling a premise. This is a considered editorial
choice, not a fact correction.

### Note B — Anecdotal Evidence sharpened

The source said: "using a personal example to determine the entire argument." This description
incorrectly implies that any use of a single case is a fallacy. A single counterexample is
*decisive* against a universal claim ("X never happens") — falsifying a universal is the correct
logical use of one case (Copi & Cohen §9.1). The fallacy is using one case to establish a
*frequency*. The film's definition ("using one case to establish a rate") isolates the actual
error without condemning the legitimate use.

### Note C — Ad Hominem narrowed

The film adds the source-reliability distinction (Beat B09) not present in the source card.
This prevents the common misuse of "ad hominem" as a shield against all credibility challenges.
The distinction is explicitly in Copi & Cohen §7.1 and is epistemically necessary for the
teaching to be useful rather than harmful.
