There are two natural ways to assign probabilities, and you have probably used both. Symmetry for equally likely outcomes, and long run frequency for stable repeatable processes. Classical probability begins with symmetry. A fair coin gives heads one of two ways. A bag with five differently colored balls gives yellow one of five ways. Favorable outcomes over total outcomes is the Laplace formula. Frequentist probability repeats a stable experiment. Seven heads in twenty tosses is seven-twentieths. As the number of trials grows, the relative frequency approaches the probability, provided the process stays stable. Constructed example, a die-rolled 60 times shows six nine times, nine sixtieths, or point one five. More stable trials should drift toward one sixth about point one six seven. Neither interpretation alone answers the deployed question. Given this specific output right now, how should belief about this one case update? Asian probability starts with a prior, observes evidence, and produces a posterior. It is natural for one-off questions. This transaction, this patient, this agent task, use whichever interpretation fits to not get tribal. Conditional probability is the engine. It reasons with partial information, a positive test, a large transaction, or a shadow in an image. P of a given B equals P of a, and B divided by P of B, provided P of B is greater than zero. The numerator is the overlap, the denominator is all of B. Conditioning shrinks the sample space. Once B happened, only outcomes in B remain possible. Among those, ask what fraction are also in A. Constructed die example, B is more than three four five six, as even. Two of the three remaining outcomes four and six are even, so P of a given B is two-thirds up from one half.
