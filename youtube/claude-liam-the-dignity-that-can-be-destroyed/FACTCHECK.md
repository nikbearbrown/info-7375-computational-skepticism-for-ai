## Correction applied before scripting

**The para. 103 quotation.** The essay renders it as:

> "entrusting an algorithm in practice with the power to select who is worthy or
> not, without anyone bearing responsibility for that judgment, is to hand over
> the task of redefining the boundaries of human possibilities."

The encyclical's own wording is:

> "entrusting an algorithm entirely with the power to declare who deserves and
> who does not means redefining the boundaries of human possibilities" — with a
> consequent loss of political and moral responsibility.

Same sense; not the same words. The essay presents it inside quotation marks.
**The film shows the encyclical's wording**, because a quotation card is a
promise that those are the words. The essay's reading of the passage is
unaffected — the argument turns on "redefining the boundaries of human
possibilities", which is verbatim in both.

## Paragraph numbers — a stated limit

Paragraph numbers up to ~95 were confirmed directly against the Vatican text.
For the later citations (99, 100, 103, 112, 117, 118, 120, 126) the **quoted
words** are verified but the **paragraph numbers** come from the essay and were
not independently confirmed. The film therefore shows the words and the document,
and does not put a paragraph number on screen for anything past 95.

## Framing discipline

The film is about a **contradiction the essay identifies**, not about Catholic
teaching being wrong. Two rules held throughout:

- Every theological claim is attributed — "the encyclical argues", "Brown reads".
  The film never asserts as its own that dignity is or is not ontological.
- The essay's own respect is preserved. It calls the foundation "secure as a
  theological claim", para. 99 "careful and largely persuasive", and the
  prescriptions "correct". A film that dropped that to sharpen the attack would
  misrepresent its source.

No claim about any living person beyond authorship. No vendor names, no model
versions, nothing that dates.
