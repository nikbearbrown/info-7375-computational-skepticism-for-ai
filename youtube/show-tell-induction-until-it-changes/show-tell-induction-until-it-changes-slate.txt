Connie Chiwa, this is Liam Inferbear with a short film for Info 7375 Computational Skepticism for AI. You might think a skeptic should throw out induction since the past can't prove the future. Don't. Induction is one of the most useful tools you have. The real question is when it breaks. Three terms induction, expecting the future to resemble the past. Stationary, a process whose statistical rules don't change over time. And ergodic, one long history speaks for the whole system. The move comes from David Hume in an inquiry concerning human understanding from 1748. The sun has risen every morning of your life. Does that prove it will rise tomorrow? Hume said no. That the sun won't rise tomorrow is no contradiction. You can picture it. The past gives no logical guarantee. But Hume didn't say stop. Only a fool or a madman he wrote would dispute the authority of experience. We can't prove the future will resemble the past and we live by it anyway. He called that custom the great guide of human life. And most of the time induction works for 10 years online shopping share of US retail sales climbed slowly and steadily from about 4% in 2010 to about 11% at the end of 2019 per the US Census Bureau. Extend the line one more quarter and you'd land close quarter after quarter. Notice what held not the number it kept rising. The rules held each quarter added a small step never more than half a point. A process whose statistical rules don't change over time is called stationary. These steps were close to it and that's what induction quietly relies on. Urgodic is the other half. In an Urgodic system following one shop over a long stretch of time gives the same average is looking across all the shops at once. That's what lets one history stand in for the whole. Then in the spring of 2020, COVID changed how people buy. Stores closed or cut back and shopping moved online. In a single quarter, online share jumped from about 12% to about 16% per the Census Bureau. A decade of small steps than one giant one. Nothing was wrong with the math, the system that made the history had changed. Hume saw that nothing forbids it. The course of nature may change, he wrote, and the past may be no rule for the future. A model trained on the old history makes the same bet. The chapter calls it a system trained on a stationary past shipped into a moving present. After the change, the model is exactly as confident as it was the day before. So keep induction. Most of the time it's the right bet. The honest rule, trust the pattern while the system that made it stays the same. When the world changes, go back and check.
