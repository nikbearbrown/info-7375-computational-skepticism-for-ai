- **"Stationary", used correctly.** NIST: a stationary process has mean, variance and autocorrelation that "do not change over time", and a trending series is not stationary (difference it). The online share rose for ten years, so it is NOT stationary and the film never says it is. B03 says "Not the number: it kept rising. The rules held", shows the differenced series (the quarterly steps) as grey bars inside one 0–0.5 point band, and says the steps "were close to" stationary. The first script draft had "These steps were stationary"; hedged before voicing.
- **"Ergodic", handled in one line (Bear used the word).** BDEFS defines it in one line ("one long history speaks for the whole system"); B04 illustrates the definition (one shop over time has the same average as all the shops at once: time average = ensemble average) and says "That's what lets one history stand in for the whole." Bear's phrasing "if the system's ergodicity has changed" is not said literally: ergodicity is defined for a process, and a structural break is better described as the process itself changing (it stops being stationary, so its old history no longer represents it). B06 therefore says "The system that made the history had changed", with Hume's "the course of nature may change … the past may be no rule for the future". Neither term gets more than one beat.
