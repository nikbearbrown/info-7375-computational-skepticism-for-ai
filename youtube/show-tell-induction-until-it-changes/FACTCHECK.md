**What this is.** Every claim the film speaks or shows, checked against `SOURCE.md` (Bear's words of 2026-09-27 and chapter 1 of INFO 7375, lines 33–97, verbatim) and, for every external fact, the primary fetched raw on 2026-09-27 into `sources/` (no WebFetch summary was used): David Hume, *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding* (1748), Sections IV–V, Selby-Bigge edition, Project Gutenberg eBook #9662 (`sources/hume-enquiry-gutenberg-9662.txt`); US Census Bureau, *Quarterly Retail E-Commerce Sales* releases for 1st quarter 2010 (CB10-64), 4th quarter 2019 (CB20-24), 1st quarter 2020 (CB20-71) and 2nd quarter 2020 (CB20-120, August 18, 2020) (`sources/census-ecomm-*.pdf` + `.txt`); the same Census series as currently revised, "E-Commerce Retail Sales as a Percent of Total Sales", seasonally adjusted, via FRED series ECOMPCTSA (source: US Census Bureau) (`sources/fred-ECOMPCTSA.csv`); NIST/SEMATECH *e-Handbook of Statistical Methods* §6.4.4.2 "Stationarity" (`sources/nist-pmc442.htm`); Wikipedia "Ergodic process" and "Ergodicity" (raw wikitext, definitions only; `sources/wiki-ergodic-process.txt`, `sources/wiki-ergodicity.txt`).

**Result.** PASS, with the COVID shift **stated at the precision both Census vintages support** and three wording points CORRECTED. (1) **The COVID buying shift** (Bear's example): the Census release of August 18, 2020 says e-commerce "accounted for 16.1 percent of total sales" in 2Q2020, up from 11.8 percent in 1Q2020 (seasonally adjusted; Table 1). The series as revised since reads 11.9 and 16.3. The film says "from about twelve percent to about sixteen, per the Census Bureau" and shows "12% → 16%", which both vintages support; the drawn line uses the current vintage. (2) **"Stationary" used correctly:** NIST defines a stationary process as one whose "mean, variance and autocorrelation structure do not change over time", and notes that a series with a trend is not stationary but can often be made so by differencing. The share itself rose for ten years, so the film does NOT call the share stationary; it says the *rules* held, shows the quarter-to-quarter steps (the differenced series: every step 2010Q1–2019Q4 between 0.0 and 0.5 points), and says those steps "were close to" stationary. (3) **"Ergodic" in one line:** said once as a definition (BDEFS: "one long history speaks for the whole system") and once as an illustration (B04: following one shop over time gives the same average as looking across all the shops at once), matching the standard definition (the time average equals the ensemble average). The film does not claim a measured ergodicity property of retail data, and it does not use Bear's phrase "the system's ergodicity has changed" literally; B06 says instead that "the system that made the history had changed" (see BUILD-LOG). Hume's words are quoted from his text, with the paragraph in each row; two quotations are fragments of longer sentences, used as he meant them. The chapter's Zillow, Google Flu Trends and turkey examples are not used (the sibling film already used the turkey and Google Flu Trends). Drawings that are not data have EXEMPT rows.
