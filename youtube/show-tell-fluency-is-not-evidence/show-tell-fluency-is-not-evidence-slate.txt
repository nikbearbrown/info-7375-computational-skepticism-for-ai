The chapter turns that into a different question. If it reads well, why do you feel so sure and sure of what? That feeling has a mechanism and the chapter calls it the fluency trap. Four terms, fluency, how smoothly and confidently a piece of prose reads. Form versus content, the shape of a sentence against what the sentence actually says. Criterion, what you decided a wrong answer would look like before you read it. In evaluation, judging what comes back against that criterion, not just reviewing and accepting. The chapter borrows two pillars from the bot speak framework, which it treats in full in its appendix A, strategic delegation, knowing what to ask the AI to do given its strengths and failure modes on this kind of task. Critical evaluation, knowing what to do with what comes back, evaluated against criteria you set before you saw it. Both matter and both recur through the book, but the chapter says plainly that neither of them protects you from the trap. That is why the trap gets its own section. Stage one, the more fluently the output is presented, the more confident you become in the output. The prose smooths out, the sentences are well formed in the needle climbs. The chapter says that part is not surprising. Stage two is the trap, the more confident you become in the output, the more confident you become in your own evaluation of it. A second needle follows the first, not because you checked anything, because the page read well. So fluency is an evaluation booster, and it boosts the wrong evaluations as readily as the right ones. It does not just make you more likely to accept a good answer. It makes you more likely to accept any answer that arrives in the same shape. The chapter's case, an agent produced a fluent, well-formed report. The email had been deleted. The account had been secured. The user read it and trusted it. When the chapter is careful here, the agent did not deceive him. A non-fluent reader would have asked the basic questions the fluent reader felt no need to ask. In the chapter's words, the fluency was the trap. The well-formed prose did the work the verification should have done. The point is not to distrust fluent outputs. On average, the chapter says, fluent outputs are more useful than non-fluent ones. The point is to not let fluency do a systemic work when you find yourself accepting an output because it sounds right, stop. When the three moves, the chapter has already given you. Ask what would have to be true for this to be wrong. That is the move from discarts. Say beforehand what a wrong answer looks like. That is the move from popper. And the third, look at the artifact, look at the world and ask what the relationship is. Why does it work on us form and content? In human speech, a well-formed sentence usually comes from someone who knows what they are talking about. When a colleague speaks in clear, organized prose, they have generally thought about it. The clarity is evidence of the thinking. The heuristic is reliable. AI systems broke it. The form comes from a statistical process that learned what well-formed prose looks like. The content is whatever that process produces from the input. The two are independent. A sentence can be maximally fluent and maximally wrong at the same time, and fluency alone cannot tell you which. The chapter says this is not a flaw that the next model will fix. It is structural. These systems learn to produce outputs that resemble correct outputs, where resemblance and correctness track each other closely, that works well. Whether they diverge in novel situations, edge cases, inputs the training data underrepresented, the system keeps producing fluent output, confidently, and exactly the shape that makes you trust it, about things that has no basis to say. The paparion move helps here. Before you read the output, specify what a wrong answer would look like, not vaguely specifically. The chapter's example, a wrong answer would assign low risk to a patient with this combination of symptoms and profile. When read it, the prior specification is your anchor. Without it, you are reading the output with no criterion except fluency, and fluency is the trap. Fluency boosts the wrong evaluations as readily as the right ones.
