**Title:** Missing Is Not One Thing · **Author:** Nik Bear Brown (narration: Liam, synthetic Kokoro voice, in for Bear) · **Lesson:** Chapter 3, the missingness paragraph of the procedural EDA pass — why the same 18% blank can be safe to fill or a trap · **Audience:** INFO 7375 students; @NikBearBrown viewers · **Status:** draft → see `BUILD-LOG.md` for the cut and the master path. Not published; no URL.

**What it is.** A `tldr` film: two TL;DR cards (what, why), the hesitant writer correcting "how much is missing" to "why it is missing", three terms, then one LEARN section in pure Manim on a constructed 50-row table. Two income columns, both 18% blank, look identical. A random wipe fills safely; table A's blanks are the under-25s, so the average lands high until you fill within the age group; table B's blanks never line up under any recorded column — until the rows are sorted by the value nobody wrote down, and the nine blanks turn out to be the nine highest earners. Only then the names: MCAR, MAR, MNAR.

**Why.** The first fix anyone reaches for is the average. The film shows, with one representation — sort the rows and watch the blanks — when that fix is safe, when a visible column rescues it, and when no plot can, so the only detector left is the question "who would not report this, and why?"
