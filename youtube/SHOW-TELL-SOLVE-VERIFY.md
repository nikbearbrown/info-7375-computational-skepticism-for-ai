## The content (the chapter's arc; keep it to the point)
1. **The folklore, and it's true.** Factoring a big number is hard; multiplying the factors back is easy. Cryptography is built on it. (State only what's plainly true; no key sizes, no numbers. Primary: any standard statement of the P vs NP / one-way-function intuition is fine as EXEMPT illustration; don't cite a specific paper unless you fetch it.)
2. **The joke: in AI deployment it inverts.** Producing the output is cheap: milliseconds, fractions of a cent. Verifying it is expensive: a clinician with twenty minutes and a stethoscope; a query against the provider's servers to compare what the agent claimed with the actual data state. Use the chapter's two examples: the triage score, and "Ash's agent's report" — say "an agent's report that an email was deleted" rather than the name Ash unless the film introduces the case (it doesn't need to). Keep the chapter's costs as it states them (fraction of a cent vs an hour of senior labor), attributed to the chapter, no invented dollar figures.
3. **Not a complaint, an observation about where the costs sit** — and will sit.
4. **If you don't budget for verification, you don't get it.** Outputs at scale; verification not at scale; every unverified output looks like a success "until one of them is the patient, or the loan, or the warrant, or the email that did not actually get deleted." Draw the pile of green checks with the one that's wrong indistinguishable from the rest.
5. **The wrong fix: automate the verification.** That's another model with the same problem, one layer up. Draw it: a checker box on top of the model box, with the same shape.
6. **The right fix: design so the verification a human can perform tells you what you need to know.** One human check placed where it counts — the system of record, not the report. This is the chapter's Figure 1.5 idea (a cost-asymmetry bar chart); if you draw bars, label them as illustrative, no values.
7. **Your Turn idea:** take one AI output you act on; write what it cost to produce and what it would cost to actually verify against the world (time, who); then name the one check a human could do in five minutes that would tell you the most. Two checks: did you price the verification honestly? Is your five-minute check against the world or against the report?
