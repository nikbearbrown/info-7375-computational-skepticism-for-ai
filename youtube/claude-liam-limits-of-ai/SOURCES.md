## Corrections / editorial decisions (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW)

1. **Challenger precision rule (B23).** The quote is attributed EXACTLY
   per the Rogers Commission record: Jerry Mason (Morton Thiokol senior
   VP) to Bob Lund (Thiokol VP of Engineering) — "take off your
   engineering hat and put on your management hat." The looser circulating
   version (NASA or unnamed management, said to Boisjoly) is never used.
   The source itself makes this correction; the episode inherits it as a
   hard rule. On-screen attribution card: "Mason, to Lund — Rogers
   Commission testimony."
2. **No accident imagery.** The Challenger beats (B22/B23) use pre-launch
   NASA imagery only (ice on the pad, the vehicle on 39B at dawn). The
   loss of the crew is neither shown nor described; the episode's subject
   is the decision meeting. Taste rule, logged as binding for pass 1/2.
3. **Real persons.** Boisjoly, Mason, Lund: named, historical public
   record (Rogers Commission). Claims kept strictly to the testimony
   record. Boisjoly's post-disclosure career referenced obliquely
   ("history records what happened") — no invented details.
4. **Named scholars, no portraits.** Harnad, Bender & Koller, Searle,
   Dennett, Boden, Elish, Guo, Rabanser/Lipton — attributed in narration
   or on citation chips; no portraits (several living).
5. **No invented numbers.** The HITL oversight study (B21) is cited
   without percentages (the source gives none). WILDS (B16) cited as
   "degrades materially" — no invented magnitudes. The clinical "94% / six
   percent at the boundary" from the underlying essay is NOT used (the
   pasted source references it only obliquely).
6. **Datable trims.** No vendor/tool names for shift-detection products;
   NIST/OECD referenced in SOURCES only, not narrated (framework names
   date less badly than products, but the beat stands without them).
