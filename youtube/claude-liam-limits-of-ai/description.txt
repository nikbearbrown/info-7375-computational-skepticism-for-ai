What the Tools Cannot Do — The Limits of AI

Three limits that aren't waiting on a bigger model. Meaning: Harnad's symbol grounding problem — a dictionary can't teach a reader who knows no language, no matter how large it grows — and the working distinction between citing a document and describing the current world. Aboutness: Searle's original-versus-derived intentionality, the removal test, and why Dennett's rejection of the whole framework lowers the bar without granting understanding. The data-world gap: distribution shift in its three flavors, WILDS's measured persistence, and Guo's finding that a model's confidence fails silently exactly at the boundary. Then the part every deployment plan waves at: the human override — Elish's moral crumple zone, the tested failure of nominal oversight, and the Challenger record, precisely attributed. An override is real only if it's independently informed, organizationally binding, and career-safe. Sometimes the honest answer is the no-deploy list.
