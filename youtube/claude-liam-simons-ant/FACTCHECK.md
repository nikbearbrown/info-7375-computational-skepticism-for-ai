## Claim 1 — Chapter placement of the ant passage

**Claim (from brief):** The ant parable is at the start of Chapter II ("The Psychology of
Thinking"), NOT Chapter I.

**Status: FLAGGED — cannot confirm chapter number without the text.**

In the first edition of *The Sciences of the Artificial* (MIT Press, 1969), the chapter order
is generally understood as:
- Chapter I: Understanding the Natural and Artificial Worlds
- Chapter II: Economic Rationality: Adaptive Artifice
- Chapter III: The Psychology of Thinking: Embedding Artifice in Nature
- Chapter IV: The Architecture of Complexity

If this ordering is correct, the ant parable (in "The Psychology of Thinking") would be in
Chapter III, not Chapter II. The brief asserts Chapter II — this may reflect a different edition
(the second edition 1981 or third edition 1996 may renumber chapters, or may have dropped
"Economic Rationality" as a separate chapter). Neither the second nor third edition chapter
ordering could be confirmed from training data with sufficient certainty.

**Narration handling:** The reel refers to "the text" and "the parable" without specifying a
chapter number. No chapter number appears in narration or on-screen text.

**Action required:** Human must verify chapter number against a physical copy or stable
scholarly citation before any on-screen text or narration asserts "Chapter II" or "Chapter III."

## Claim 6 — Wehner / Cataglyphis path integration

**Claim (from brief):** Desert ants path-integrate using celestial compass plus internal
odometer, with systematic search when displaced. Verify specific findings before quoting numbers.

**Status: VERIFIED (general claims); FLAGGED (specific numbers).**

General findings, well-established:
- Cataglyphis ants (North African desert ants) use a sky-polarization compass ✓
- They maintain a running "home vector" using path integration (dead reckoning) ✓
- When displaced, they head in the direction the home vector predicts, then execute a
  systematic spiral/arc search when they fail to find the nest ✓
- Key researcher: Rüdiger Wehner (University of Zurich), decades of work from the 1980s onward ✓

Specific numbers (distances traveled, error tolerances, search radii): these vary by experiment
and cannot be cited without access to specific Wehner papers. They are NOT in narration.

**Narration handling:** The three general findings (celestial compass, odometric accumulation,
systematic displaced-search) are stated at the level of established fact. No numbers, no specific
paper citations.

## Claim 7 — The "scissors" metaphor

**Claim (from brief):** NOT in the ant/psychology chapters of *Sciences of the Artificial*.
If used, trace to Simon's 1990 Science paper.

**Status: OMITTED from narration.**

The scissors metaphor ("cognition is like a pair of scissors; two blades are the task
environment and the computational capabilities of the actor") is attributed to Simon but its
precise source is contested in the scholarly literature — candidates include his 1978 AEA
presidential address, *Reason in Human Affairs* (1983), and other sources. The brief flags that
it is NOT in the ant/psychology chapters, and points to a 1990 Science paper as a possible
source. A Simon 1990 Science paper on altruism selection ("A Mechanism for Social Selection
and Successful Altruism") does not obviously contain the scissors metaphor; this may be a
different 1990 paper or the attribution may be imprecise.

**Decision: the scissors metaphor does not appear in this reel.** The narrative does not need
it — the ant parable, the formalism, and the simulation cover the same conceptual ground more
concretely.

## Claim 8 — "Satisficing" — chapter placement in Sciences of the Artificial

**Claim (from brief):** Satisficing is coined in Chapter III's design-science material — cite
it there, not to the ant.

**Status: OMITTED from narration.**

Satisficing was introduced by Simon in "Rational Choice and the Structure of the Environment"
(*Psychological Review*, 1956) and *Administrative Behavior* (1947/1957), well before *Sciences
of the Artificial* (1969). The claim that it is "coined" in Chapter III of *Sciences of the
Artificial* appears to mean that it is used/defined there — but the original coinage is in
earlier work. The chapter number cannot be confirmed without text access (see ambiguity in
Claim 1). More importantly, satisficing does not appear in the arc of this reel; it belongs
to Simon's design-science / bounded-rationality work, which is adjacent but not the focus here.

**Decision: satisficing does not appear in this reel.**
