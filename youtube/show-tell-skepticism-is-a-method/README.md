# Skepticism Is a Method, Not a Mood

**What this is.** A show-tell film (about 2:54) for INFO 7375, Computational Skepticism for AI, from chapter 1's section "What I mean by skepticism". Liam, in for Bear, says it is a short film for the course, then makes one point: skepticism is a method you perform on a claim, and its enemy is not laziness but confidence, the output that looks right. Three moves, one per philosopher, each drawn on one cast. Descartes: ask what would have to be true for the claim to be wrong, and the answers become a checklist (Knight Capital put new code on eight servers, missed one, and lost more than $460 million in about 45 minutes, per the SEC). Hume: confidence belongs to the model, not the world (the turkey fed for a thousand days, surprised on day 1,001; Google Flu Trends too high in 100 of 108 weeks, per *Science*). Popper: a claim that can't be wrong isn't a claim, and one black swan beats any count of white ones (a sepsis model used in hundreds of US hospitals missed about two of every three cases when outside researchers finally tested it, per *JAMA Internal Medicine*). The close: you don't have to believe the philosophers; the moves are tools, like a load test on a beam. Your Turn hands the viewer a prompt that runs all three moves on one AI answer they rely on. **Why.** Bear asked for it on 2026-09-27 for the course; it is the week-one idea of the book in under three minutes. **Found.** The chapter is right about every mechanism but carries two wrong figures the film had to correct: Knight's loss (the SEC says more than $460 million, not "about $440 million") and the turkey's day (day 1,001, not "day one thousand").
