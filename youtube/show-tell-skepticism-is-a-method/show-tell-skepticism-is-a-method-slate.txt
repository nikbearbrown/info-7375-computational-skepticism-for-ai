Hello, this is Liam in For Bear with a short film for Info 7375 Computational Skepticism for AI. You might think skepticism is just doubting everything. That's a mood. Here, skepticism is a method, a set of moves you run on a claim. Three terms, radical doubt, asking what would make a claim wrong. Induction, trusting a pattern because it held in the past, and falsifiable. A claim that names the result that would prove it wrong. Not the eye role, not the person who is against everything. A method, a few moves you perform on a claim. They can be taught and you can see whether someone did them. Three philosophers each donate one move. The hard part isn't doubting what looks suspicious. It's doubting what looks right, fluent, specific, neatly formatted. That's when the moves matter most and when the urge to skip them is strongest. The enemy isn't laziness, it's confidence. Move one is from day-cart, take the claim and ask what would have to be true for this to be wrong. The answers aren't a feeling. They're a checklist. The training data, the inputs, the setting it runs in. Each one you can check. In 2012, the trading firm Knight Capital put new code on eight servers. A technician missed one and no one noticed. On that eight server, old code unused since 2003 woke up. In about 45 minutes, it made over 4 million trades and Knight lost more than $460 million per the SEC. Ask what would make done wrong and you check all eight. Move two is from Hume, a model that was right a thousand times has no guarantee about the next time. Picture a turkey, fed every morning for a thousand days. Each meal makes it sure of the next. On day one thousand and one, the farmer comes with an axe. Big systems do it too. Google FluTrends estimated flu from web searches. From August 2011, it ran too high in 100 of 108 weeks per a paper in science. A turkey with a spreadsheet. Its confidence belongs to the model, not to the world. Move three is from popper, a claim that can't be wrong isn't a claim yet. And piling up successes never proves one. No count of white swans proves that all swans are white. One black swan disproves it. So hunt for the black swan. A sepsis alert model was running in hundreds of US hospitals without an adequate outside test. When outside researchers ran one on 27,697 patients, it missed about two of every three sepsis cases. You don't have to believe any of these philosophers. The moves are tools like a load test on a beam. The test finds a flaw or it comes back clean. Either way, you've learned something.
